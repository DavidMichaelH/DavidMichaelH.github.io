Coupling and Total Variation

Theorem

Let \(P,Q\) denote probability measures on a common measurable space \((\Omega,\Sigma)\). Then \[ \mathsf{TV}(P,Q) = \inf_{\pi} \mathbb{P}(X \not = Y) .\] where the infimum is taken over all couplings \(\pi\) of \(P\) and \(Q\). Moreover, the infimum is achieved.

Proof

We explictly construct a coupling of \((X,Y)\) as follows. Conveniently define the measure \[ \lambda(A) = \int_A \min \{d \mu , d \nu \} \] where \(\lambda(\Omega) = 1 - \mathsf{TV}(\mu,\nu)\). L et \(\overline{\lambda}\) be the measure \(\lambda\) normalized to have total mass 1. Define the measures \[ \overline{P}(A) = \dfrac{P(A) - \lambda(A)}{1 - \lambda(\Omega)} \] \[ \overline{Q}(A) = \dfrac{Q(A) - \lambda(A)}{1 - \lambda(\Omega)} \] Then, we can define the coupling \((X,Y)\) as follows. First, with probability \(1- \mathsf{TV}(P,Q)\) we have \(X=Y\). In this case sample from the measure \(\overline{\lambda}\). Otherwise, sample \(X\) from \(\overline{P}\) and \(Y\) from \(\overline{Q}\) independently. We see next that this coupling achieves the desired result.

Clearly \(\mathbb{P}(X \not = Y) = 1 - \mathsf{TV}(P,Q)\) by construction. Hence, it remains to show that the marginals of \((X,Y)\) are \(P\) and \(Q\).

Then, we have that \[ P(X \in A) = (1- \mathsf{TV}(P,Q)) \cdot \overline{\lambda}(A) + \mathsf{TV}(P,Q) \cdot \overline{P}(A) \] \[ = \int_A \min \{d \mu , d \nu \} + P(A) - \int_A \min \{d \mu , d \nu \} \] \[ = P(A) .\] The proof for \(Q\) is similar. This completes the proof.
